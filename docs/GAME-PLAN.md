# Ark Pals: Game & Content Plan (v0.1)

How Ark Pals grows from a 30-minute game into one a family plays for months. It builds on
[BUSINESS-PLAN.md](BUSINESS-PLAN.md): §6 sets the launch content bar, §7 the rollout. This plan says what to build and in what order, how to keep the quality up as content multiplies, and what pace to work at.

---

## 1. Where we are

There are six story islands. Each one is:
- a 6–7 page story
- 2–3 activities
- a memory verse
- a battle
- a reward

That's about **5 minutes an island**. Carter went through most of the game in **30 minutes**.

Around the islands sit the Ark modes: 18 Pals, the Pal Kitchen, Pal homes, coloring, stickers, the egg, sing-along, bedtime and the birthday party. These are good reasons to come back, but they can't stand in for the adventure.

Quality problems keep slipping through, and they're two kinds:
- **Art defects.** For example, the Noah dove had one wing growing out of its back.
- **Emoji that don't match the narration.** "Day five, fish and birds" shows a lone 🐟. "Baby Jesus in the stable" shows an empty hut. Emoji also look different on every device.

Both come from the same cause. Each picture is drawn once, by hand, and nothing checks it against what the narrator says.

## 3. The campaign

### 3.1 Seas, islands, visits

The map becomes a voyage through the Bible. **Each sea is one era, holding 4–6 islands**, and the Ark sails from sea to sea. Finished islands keep their flags. Carter keeps everything she's done when the map is reordered.

| Sea | Islands (✓ = built) |
|---|---|
| 1. In the Beginning | Creation ✓, Noah ✓, Abraham's Stars, Joseph's Coat |
| 2. Out of Egypt | Baby Moses, the Burning Bush, the Red Sea, Manna in the Desert |
| 3. The Promised Land | Jericho, Ruth & Naomi, Samuel Listens, David & Goliath ✓ |
| 4. Kings & Prophets | Elijah, Esther, Daniel & the Lions, Jonah ✓ |
| 5. Jesus Comes | Baby Jesus ✓, Boy Jesus at the Temple, Fishers of People, Jesus Calms the Storm |
| 6. Jesus' Stories & Miracles | Loaves & Fishes ✓, the Lost Sheep, the Good Samaritan, Zacchaeus |
| 7. Easter & Beyond | Palm Sunday, Easter Morning, Pentecost |

That's 27 islands. The order is chronological, so the voyage is the Bible's own story. Easter falls on March 28, 2027, so the Easter island should ship by mid-March.

**Every island has three visits of about 8–10 minutes each:**

1. **The story.**
   - Story part 1, as a living picture book (§5.1).
   - The island's own signature mini-game (§5.2).
   - One learning activity.
   - It ends on a gentle cliffhanger: *"Will the dove find dry land? Let's find out next time!"*
2. **The adventure.**
   - Story part 2.
   - Two learning activities.
   - The memory verse.
3. **The rescue.**
   - Put the story in order, using its own pictures.
   - The friendly battle, where the island's Pal joins the Ark.
   - The celebration and a **song spot**: the island's song, added to the sing-along.

### 3.2 Pacing for a mass-market game

**Daily voyage.**
- The Ark sails to **two new visits a day** by default.
- After that, the crew rests. Everything else stays open: replays with fresh questions, the Ark, the kitchen, songs, coloring, stickers and bedtime.
- Parents set the limit in the Parent Corner, from 1 a day up to no limit.

This spaces the learning out, matches the existing 60-minute reminder, and makes content last without padding it.

**Stars.** Each island earns 1–3 stars for first-try answers. Replays are where the stars come from, and three stars on a whole sea earns its golden flag.

**Daily reasons to come back:**
- hungry Pals
- the egg
- Pal requests ("Ember wants to play catch!")
- the countdown before a birthday
- seasonal events (Christmas, Easter, birthdays, the start of summer)

### 3.3 A story that ties it together

**Grumbleshade**, the pouty shadow from every battle, gets an arc:
- Island by island, the child learns why he's grumpy: he thinks nobody could love him.
- On Easter Morning, he finds out God loves him too, and becomes a Pal, *Gladshade*.

This is grace first, and it gives the whole voyage an ending worth reaching.

**The Ark grows with the voyage.** New decks open as Pals join: a garden, a kitchen and a music room. Pals live in rooms by their fruit of the Spirit, and the child decorates them. Filling a fruit's room plays a celebration.

## 4. Learning backbone

Each sea carries the next learning goals. The adaptive levels keep meeting each child where they are, and a short placement game at the start sets the levels.

| Sea | Reading | Math |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | letter sounds, rhyming | counting to 20, shapes |
| 2 | blending CVC words | more/fewer, adding within 10 |
| 3 | sight words, word families | taking away within 10, patterns |
| 4 | short sentences (picture match) | ± within 20, number line |
| 5 | reading a sentence and answering | tens and ones, time to the hour |
| 6–7 | tiny stories with questions | money (coins), half hours, measuring |

New activity types carry these goals: word builder, sentence builder, number line hop, clock, market (coins), and longer/shorter.

## 5. Interactivity

### 5.1 Living picture books

**Every story page gets 2–4 things to tap.** Tapping a lion makes it roar and shake its mane. Tapping the rainbow makes it shimmer. Tapping Noah makes him say *"God told me to build a boat!"*

The hotspots are marked in the art and listed per page, so this costs very little per page. Kids replay stories to find them all.

### 5.2 Signature mini-games

Each island gets one game that only makes sense there. They're built from a reusable set of mechanics:

| Mechanic | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Build it** (drag parts into place) | the ark, the tabernacle tent, the manger, Nehemiah's wall |
| **Catch it** | manna falling, fish into Peter's net, rain into jars (with counting) |
| **Steer it** | the reed basket down the Nile, the boat through Jonah's storm, the sheep home |
| **Rhythm** | marching round Jericho 7 times, Miriam's tambourine |
| **Spot it** (find things in a big picture) | the lost sheep, Zacchaeus in the tree |
| **Share it** (fair shares, early division) | the loaves and fishes, Ruth's grain |
| **Choose kindly** | the Good Samaritan: who stops to help? (grace-first feedback) |
| **Paint it** (color by number) | Joseph's coat |

### 5.3 The buddy Pal as guide

The child's own Pal pops into stories and activities to react, cheer and give hints. One character, already personalized, makes the whole game feel like *their* adventure.

## 6. Characters

- **Bible people** get a fixed model sheet in `art/people.tsx`, so Moses, Mary and Jesus look the same on every island. Nobody is redrawn from scratch.
- **Pals:**
  - one new Pal per island
  - seasonal Pals: Christmas, Easter, birthday
  - egg Pals
  - **30+ at launch**, each with a personality line, a favorite food and a room on the Ark
- **Grumbleshade** has the arc in §3.3.
- **The family** (§3.1 of the business plan) appears in the birthday and bedtime stories. Later it can appear in "you are there" moments, like the child's own family in the crowd at the loaves and fishes.

## 7. Art: more drawn art, fewer emoji, and fewer defects

**Keep the code-drawn SVG art.** It's sharp at any size and tiny to download. It animates, and it works offline.

Above all, it's **personal**. The child and their family appear in the pictures, drawn with their own look. Painted or AI-generated images can't do that, so they won't be used for shipped art. They're fine for sketching ideas.

**What we'll build:**

1. **An item library (`art/items`)** to replace every emoji in activities.
   - About 150 drawn pictures to start: animals, food, objects, nature and Bible things.
   - Each activity item names its picture, and the narrator says exactly what it shows.
   - "Fish and birds" will show fish *and* birds.
   - The 57 emoji used today come first.
2. **Story cards.** Activities that retell a story (put in order, quizzes) use small versions of that story's own pages, so the activity always matches the story.
3. **A shared kit.** Backgrounds, props, people and animals are drawn once and reused everywhere. The dove fix this week fixed it in three stories at once.

**A quality gate every island passes before it ships:**
- Every page rendered at 2×.
- An AI reviewer checks the renders for anatomy, overlaps, counts and match with the narration. That's this week's pass, made routine.
- A motion check and the existing automated tests.
- A grown-up reads it once on a review page that shows each picture beside its narration.

## 8. Technology

**Keep:** React, TypeScript, Vite, the installable web app, SVG and CSS animation, Web Audio and our drag engine. They work well on iPads, stay small, and run offline.

**Add:**

| What | Why |
|---|---|
| **Load each sea when it's needed** (code splitting) | The game grows from 6 islands to 27+ without a slower first load. The current sea and the next stay cached for offline play. |
| **Automated playthroughs in Safari's engine** (Playwright with WebKit) | A robot plays every visit on every deploy and compares screenshots. iPads run WebKit, so it catches Safari-only bugs too. |
| **Island scaffold** (`npm run new-island moses`) | New content starts from a template and is mostly data, as step 4 of the business plan asks. |
| **Review page** (staging only) | Every page and activity for one island in one place, so a grown-up can proofread it on an iPad. |
| **Privacy-first play stats** (first-party, totals only, parent opt-in, no outside services) | Shows where kids stop, replay or struggle, so the pacing can be tuned for the beta (COPPA-friendly, §5 of the business plan). |
| **Narration clips per island** (step 2 voice pipeline) | Stored with each island and fetched as needed, instead of all at once. |

**Later, only if it pays off:**
- Rive, for richer character animation, if a human animator joins.
- Capacitor, for App Store packaging. That needs a decision about Apple's in-app purchase rules versus Stripe.

**Not recommended:** rewriting in a game engine (Unity, Godot, Phaser). We'd throw away working code and the personalized art, and get a heavier app. Our play is drag, tap and draw, and the web handles that well. A canvas can be dropped in for a particle-heavy mini-game if one ever needs it.

## 9. Pace and order

**Week 1: the quality pass.** Started.
- Fix everything the audit finds: art, narration, emoji and Pals.
- Item library v1 for the 57 activity emoji, and story cards in put-in-order activities.
- The review page.

**Weeks 2–3: the campaign framework.**
- Seas and the reordered map (progress kept).
- Visits, stars and the daily voyage.
- Living-page hotspots.
- The buddy Pal as guide.
- Loading each sea when needed.
- Then rebuild the six existing islands into three visits each, with story part 2, two new activities, a signature mini-game and a song spot.

**Weeks 4–10: content waves.**
- About **3 new islands a week**, each through the quality gate.
- Several islands are drafted in parallel (story, data, art), with one integrator reviewing and fixing.
- **The parent review of each island's script is the real limit on speed.**
- First wave: Moses & the Red Sea, Daniel, Joseph, Abraham, then Easter well before March 28.

**In parallel, from the business plan:**
- **Voice (step 2):** build the studio now, but have Mom record **after** an island's script is final, or she'll record lines twice.
- **The multi-family server (step 3)** isn't needed until the church beta.

**Progress (Oct 4, 2026):**
- **The quality pass and the campaign framework are done.** Every island has three visits, a mini-game and a song spot. The review page, item library, seas, daily voyage, hotspots and buddy Pal are all in.
- **Content waves: 24 islands are live.** Each wave is built by parallel agents, read by a fresh reviewer, fixed, voiced, deployed and checked in production before the next one starts.
  - Wave 1: the six islands deepened, plus Abraham, Joseph, the Red Sea and Daniel.
  - Wave 2: Baby Moses, The Burning Bush and Manna, which finish the sea "Out of Egypt".
  - Wave 3: The Walls of Jericho, Ruth and Naomi, and Samuel Listens.
  - Wave 4: Elijah and Queen Esther, which finish "Kings & Prophets", and Boy Jesus at the Temple.
  - Wave 5: Fishers of People and Jesus Calms the Storm, which finish "Jesus Comes", and The Lost Sheep.
  - Wave 6: The Good Samaritan and Zacchaeus, which finish "Jesus' Stories & Miracles".
  - Next: wave 7, Palm Sunday, Easter Morning and Pentecost (their songs are already made), well before Easter. That finishes all seven seas.
- **Each island loads when it's needed**, so the game starts with 38% less to download.
- **The multi-family server came early, at Carter's request.** Families went live Oct 4.
  - Grown-ups join by an invitation link, with their own avatars, and each family's data is its own.
  - Sign-in links and invitations can also come by email, once the domain's email is set up (deploy/DEPLOY.md).
  - Each family can see what's kept, download all of it, or delete its account.
- **Voice:** Mom's recording script is ready. Grok narration is made ahead of time for every fixed line, including the lines the game builds from names (the map, battles, memory verses, a Pal growing up), so the device voice is only a fallback.

**Why this order:** if 20 islands were built on today's thin template, they'd inherit its problems (emoji, short islands, art drawn once and never checked). Fixing the template first makes every later island longer, more interactive and better checked, at no extra cost per island.

## 10. Decisions (all approved, Oct 3, 2026)

1. **Visits and the daily voyage:**
   - Each island has 3 visits.
   - By default, 2 new visits a day. Parents can change this or turn it off.
   - Islands keep a single visit until they're deepened to three, so a day never runs short.
2. **Bible order:** the map is reordered into the seas in chronological order. Every player keeps the islands they've already finished.
3. **Island songs:** new lyrics to public-domain tunes, as with "Noah Built a Big Boat".
4. **Play stats:** privacy-first stats for the church beta, opt-in and first-party, with totals only.
5. **Art:** code-drawn art behind the review gate. We'll revisit a human illustrator after the beta.
