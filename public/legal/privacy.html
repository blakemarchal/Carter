Ark Pals Privacy Policy

DRAFT for legal review. Written to match exactly what the game and server do as of October 2026, and published at arkpals.world/privacy so it can be read in place; not final until reviewed. Items in [brackets] are still to confirm.

Ark Pals is a Bible adventure game for children ages 4 to 7, made by Marchal Technologies LLC ("we"). This policy says what we keep, why, who else sees it, and how a family can see or delete all of it.

In short: grown-ups sign in, not children. We keep only what the game needs to work for your family. We never show ads, never sell or rent information, use no third-party tracking or analytics, and nothing is shared between families. No child's name ever leaves our server. A parent can download or delete everything at any time in the Parent Corner.

Who uses Ark Pals

Accounts belong to families, and only grown-ups sign in. A parent starts a family and invites other grown-ups (a grandparent, the other parent) with a one-time link. Children play on a device a grown-up has signed in; they never type an email address or create an account, and there's no chat or any way to contact other people.

What we keep, and why

On your device (in the app's own storage, which stays on the device):

  • each player's first name or nickname, a picture they choose, how their character looks, and optionally their birthday, so the game can greet them and hold their birthday party;
  • the family names a parent adds for the stories (what the children call their grown-ups, brothers, sisters and pets);
  • each player's progress, Pals, stickers and settings;
  • copies of the game itself, narration clips, the house narrator's recordings and songs, so it works offline.

On our server, for your family only:

  • your family's name, and each grown-up's name, role (parent or grown-up), chosen avatar, and email address if they add one (to be sent a sign-in link);
  • for a family that signed up on our website: when the parent agreed to these terms for their children, as our record of their consent (it's kept with the family, and deleted with it);
  • the devices signed in to your family: a name like "iPad", and when each was first and last used;
  • backups of your players' progress (copies of what's on each device, so a lost or reset device doesn't lose it): the newest 60 are kept;
  • voice recordings a grown-up makes in Family voices (story pages, and their children's names, so the game can say a name in their own voice), and songs a grown-up adds, for your family's devices to play.

Names families use, counted: so our narrator can record the children's names families use that aren't in her name library yet, we count how many families use each child's first name (read from the players and brothers and sisters in each family's newest progress backup). We (the site owner) see a name only when three or more families use it, and then only the name and how many families, never which families. Nothing is kept: the count is made when we look.

Play totals, only if a parent turns them on (Parent Corner, "Help make Ark Pals better"; off until then, and set on each device separately). They help us see where children stop, replay or find things hard, so we can make the game better. Exactly this is kept:

  • for each day (the date only, never the time), how many times each island, each visit of an island, and each kind of activity in a visit (a story, a quiz, a maze…) was started, finished and replayed (started again by a player who had already finished that island), and how many questions in each activity were answered wrong the first time;
  • for each day, in practice rounds: how many times each kind of learning exercise (building a word or a sentence, the number line, the clock, coins, measuring) and the questions of each skill (reading, numbers) were started, finished and got right the first time (with no wrong tries and no help), at each level (1 to 10);
  • for each day, how many times the first-time treasure hunt (which finds where to start reading and numbers) ended at each level, for each skill (one count per skill when the hunt is finished);
  • nothing else: no names, no players or which player did what, no devices, no answers, no free text and no times of day. Counts from all the devices that share are added together into one set of totals for your family.

They're kept on our server with the rest of your family's data, for 400 days (about 13 months), after which each day's counts are deleted. They're in your family's download, deleted when the family is deleted, and a parent can delete them at any time ("Delete our play totals"). Turning sharing off on a device stops it counting and drops anything it hasn't sent yet. We (the site owner) look only at sums across every family that shares, never at one family's totals, and no sums are shown at all until at least five families share, so one family's play can't be picked out. They're never shared with anyone else or used for advertising.

Voice coverage (our own devices only): when one of our devices (the site owner's) hears a line in the device's voice because no recording or Grok clip had it yet, it tells our server the line, so we can make it. Never a line with anyone's name in it, and nothing from any other family's devices.

Sign-in: one cookie, set by us, keeps a signed-in device signed in. It isn't used for anything else.

We don't collect location, contacts, photos or anything else from the device, and the game makes no requests to anyone else's servers: its fonts, pictures, music and everything else come from ours.

Who else handles it

We use a few service providers, only to run the game:

  • DigitalOcean hosts our server in the United States, where everything above is stored.
  • xAI makes the narrator's voice (Grok's "Ara" voice), on our server: the game's own lines, made ahead of time; the pieces lines are put together from (numbers, letters, the names of things and Pals, and the children's first names in our name library, which is the US Social Security Administration's public list of common baby names); and, the first time one is needed, a game line that's put together during play, like "Number four, green!". Those lines are the game's own words and never contain a name from your family. Nothing from your family is sent to xAI unless a parent chooses it, and no child's name ever leaves our server otherwise. As our own family records the lines, their voice replaces Grok's. A child's name that isn't in the library is left out of the line ("Way to go!" instead of "Way to go, Ella!"), unless a parent turns on "Let the narrator say <name>" (Parent Corner, under the player; off until then). Only then is that one name, and nothing else, sent to xAI, once, to make two short clips of the narrator saying it, which are kept with your family's recordings, are in your download, and are deleted when you turn it off or delete the family.
  • Resend sends our emails (sign-in links and invitations) when a grown-up asks for one; it receives that email address and the email.
  • ImprovMX forwards email sent to hello@arkpals.world to us.
  • Stripe handles payments when a family chooses a plan, and its checkout, Link, is the seller of record for the payment: it works out and collects sales tax, and sends the receipts. Stripe receives the email of the parent who starts the plan, what they enter on Stripe's own checkout page (card and billing address; a Grace code's checkout asks for no card), and our id for the family, and nothing about the children. A parent can choose to save their details with Link, under Link's own privacy policy. We never see card numbers. We keep the family's Stripe customer id, plan, status and paid-through date; they're in the family's download, and deleting the family deletes its Stripe customer too, which ends the plan (Stripe keeps its own record of past payments, as the law requires).

We don't sell, rent or trade information, and we share it with no one else, except if required by law.

Children's privacy

Ark Pals is made for children, so we follow the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA). We collect the least we can: a child's first name or nickname, how their character looks, an optional birthday, their progress, and any recordings their grown-ups choose to make. All of it is entered by a parent or played on a device a parent signed in. [To confirm with counsel: how a parent gives consent. At paid launch, a parent's card payment; for families on a Grace code or the free beta, a confirmation email and a consent checkbox.]

A parent can review everything we keep about their family, download it, or delete it (below), and can stop us collecting more at any time by deleting the family.

Seeing, downloading and deleting your family's data

In the Parent Corner, under "Your family's data", a parent can:

  • see what's kept, and how much room is left for recordings and songs;
  • download everything as one file: your grown-ups and devices, every progress backup, every recording and every song, and your play totals (if you share them);
  • delete the family's account, which removes every grown-up's sign-in, every device, and all your backups, recordings, songs and play totals from our server right away. (What's on each device stays on that device until the app is removed.)

Grown-ups can also leave a family, and parents can remove grown-ups or sign devices out. For anything else, write to hello@arkpals.world.

How long we keep it

  • Your family's information stays until you delete it or the family is deleted.
  • Progress backups: the newest 60 for each family.
  • Play totals (only if a parent turned them on): 400 days, then each day's counts are deleted.
  • Invitation and sign-in links stop working after 30 minutes to 30 days, depending on the kind, and once used.
  • Backups of our server's data, in case of an accident: kept for 30 days. [Copies made just before an update: to be deleted after 30 days too, once that cleanup is set up.]
  • [To decide before launch: a family's data is deleted 90 days after its plan ends, if the family doesn't come back.]

Keeping it safe

Everything travels over an encrypted connection (HTTPS). Sign-in links work once and are stored only in scrambled form; sign-in cookies can't be read by scripts. Each family's files are kept apart. Only we can sign in to the server, with a key (never a password); its firewall lets in only web traffic and that sign-in. It gets security updates automatically, and its data is backed up every night.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes in a way that matters, we'll say so in the game, and by email to the family's parents, before it takes effect. Questions or requests: hello@arkpals.world. Marchal Technologies LLC, [mailing address].
